\begin{abstract}
Large language models can propose rich experimental plans, but deploying those
plans requires a scorer whose rankings survive contact with realized task loss.
We study this link in multi-source location finding with an explicit likelihood.
An entropy-based StrategyEIG scorer ranks realized entropy reduction
($\rho=0.38$--$0.44$), yet is nearly uncorrelated with realized localization
RMSE and does not beat naive or greedy-EIG execution. We then test the natural
repair: replace entropy with expected posterior permutation-matched RMSE. A
pre-registered, no-LLM replay over 60 standard-task probe states fails its
proceed gate: the depth-1 task-loss scorer reaches macro Spearman
$\rho=0.231$ against realized posterior-risk reduction, below the $\approx0.3$
threshold. Increasing rollout integration to 1024 samples and repairing
particle-support coupling do not recover the gate. We therefore stop before
policy deployment. The result is a measured negative finding: task-aligned
utilities are not sufficient when posterior-predictive plan values weakly rank
truth-conditional outcomes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

LLM-driven Bayesian experimental design (BED) separates an attractive pair of
capabilities. A language model can express a reusable strategy in natural
language, while an explicit simulator can score the strategy before execution.
This separation promises non-myopic planning without training a task-specific
policy or expanding a full LLM action tree. It also creates a fragile link: the
quantity that is easy to estimate in simulation may not rank the quantity the
user ultimately cares about.

Information gain is the standard BED objective. For localization, however,
posterior concentration is not the same as localization accuracy. A posterior
can lose entropy by concentrating on the wrong branch of a multimodal source
configuration. This is not merely a philosophical distinction: in our banked
Path A experiments, entropy-scored plans rank realized entropy and
truth-log-posterior quality, while their correlation with point RMSE is near
zero and the resulting StrategyEIG policy trails naive and greedy EIG.

This paper asks whether replacing entropy with a task-aligned posterior loss
repairs that first decision link. We use expected posterior
permutation-matched RMSE, which is available at decision time and never accesses
the hidden source configuration. Before implementing or deploying an
arbitration policy, we pre-register a local ranking-fidelity gate on existing
standard-task candidate roots. The gate fails, including after rollout-count,
horizon, and support-resampling diagnostics. Following the pre-registration,
we do not launch the downstream policy pilot.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A two-link diagnostic that separates scorer-to-posterior-objective
  fidelity from posterior-objective-to-task-loss rankability.
  \item A measured entropy/task-loss mismatch for LLM-scaffolded experimental
  design, supported by paired Path A policy results.
  \item A no-LLM test of expected posterior RMSE as a repair on standard
  multi-source location finding, including convergence and support checks.
  \item A negative-result protocol in which a failed scorer gate prevents an
  expensive policy claim rather than being hidden by a downstream sweep.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Bayesian experimental design.}
Deep adaptive design amortizes sequential BED policies and evaluates total
information gain with contrastive bounds \citep{foster2021deep}. Classical
decision-theoretic design also permits utilities defined by downstream loss
rather than entropy. Our setting keeps the location likelihood explicit so that
the utility question can be isolated from learned-posterior error.

\paragraph{LLM-driven design.}
BED-LLM uses language models for intelligent information gathering
\citep{choudhury2025bedllm}. Brute-force LLM lookahead can be non-myopic but
grows rapidly with depth. Strategy-level rollout instead asks an LLM to propose
natural-language policies and uses simulation to rank them. We focus on the
validity of that ranking, not on claiming a new decision-theoretic utility.

\paragraph{Online planning.}
Constrained BED and informative path planning optimize explicit transition
models. COPEx is directly adjacent \citep{guo2026constrained}. The constrained
Path A environment in this paper is diagnostic background: its purpose is to
show that a planning gap can exist while an LLM scaffold still fails to exploit
it. The Path B gate itself uses standard unconstrained multi-source location
finding and no environment engineering.

\section{Diagnostic Framework}

\subsection{Location Posterior And Task Loss}

A hidden configuration $\theta$ contains two source locations in $\mathbb R^2$.
At query $x$, the signal is an inverse-square sum with multiplicative log-normal
noise. A finite particle support $\{\theta_i\}$ is reweighted with the exact
likelihood. Source labels are exchangeable, so configurations are canonically
ordered before averaging and all RMSEs use best-permutation matching.

For posterior $p$, let $\hat\theta(p)$ be its canonical posterior mean. We define
the decision-time posterior risk
\[
  L(p)=\mathbb E_{\theta\sim p}
  \left[\operatorname{RMSE}_{\rm perm}(\theta,\hat\theta(p))\right].
\]
For a candidate root query $x$, the estimated utility is the Monte Carlo
posterior-predictive reduction
\[
  \widehat U(x)=L(p_t)-
  \frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^R L(p_{t+1}^{(r)}).
\]
This utility is task-aligned but truth-free: simulated truths are sampled from
$p_t$. The realized smooth target fixes the recorded hidden truth, generates
paired observation noise, updates the same exact-likelihood posterior, and
measures the resulting reduction in $L$. We also report realized point-RMSE
reduction to the fixed truth.

\subsection{Gate 0 Protocol}

We reuse 60 probe records from 20 trials at history lengths 0, 3, and 6. Each
record contains the hidden state, realized history, and 6--10 LLM-proposed
strategy roots. Legacy records do not contain rollout posterior snapshots or
future query trajectories, so we use the pre-specified local fallback: score
the stored roots with the analytic location model and no LLM calls. The
canonical result uses depth 1, where there is no continuation-policy mismatch.

The posterior approximation begins with a common pool of 100,000 normal-prior
particles, conditions on each history, systematically resamples 256 particles,
and applies likelihood-targeted MCMC rejuvenation. Candidate comparisons share
random controls. The canonical scorer uses 256 posterior-predictive rollouts and
the smooth target averages 256 truth-conditional deployments.

For each probe we compute Spearman correlation across candidate roots and then
macro-average over probes. The pre-registered proceed threshold is
$\rho\gtrsim0.3$ at a tested horizon. We additionally report (i) the old entropy
scorer against the new smooth target where available, (ii) task scorer against
point-RMSE reduction, and (iii) the rankability ceiling: realized smooth-target
reduction against realized point-RMSE reduction. If the task scorer fails, no
arbitration policy or cluster pilot is run.

\subsection{Path A Motivation}

Path A evaluates entropy-scored StrategyEIG on a locality-constrained
branch-decoy environment. It uses common rollout controls, fixed-support
analytical posterior updates, support-grid candidate generation, and
matched-compute myopic controls. Thirty paired trials compare naive, greedy EIG,
and StrategyEIG depths 1, 3, and 5. A second 30-trial arm removes the movement
constraint. These runs motivate the repair; they are not evidence for the Path B
task-loss method.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Entropy Fidelity Does Not Imply Localization Fidelity}

The 26B A4B entropy scorer appears successful under an information-only gate.
Across depths 2, 3, and 5, estimated-vs-realized entropy Spearman correlations
are $0.380\pm0.045$, $0.440\pm0.041$, and $0.396\pm0.044$; corresponding
truth-log-posterior correlations are about 0.36--0.37. Yet point-RMSE
correlations are $-0.088\pm0.051$, $-0.080\pm0.045$, and
$-0.035\pm0.050$. The scorer ranks its own information objective but not the
localization endpoint.

The paired policy sweep confirms that this disconnect matters. In the
constrained arm, final RMSE means are 0.166 for naive, 0.412 for greedy EIG,
0.542 for StrategyEIG-d5, 0.929 for d1, and 1.127 for d3. Depth 5 improves over
shorter StrategyEIG and matched-compute myopic controls, but it does not beat
either baseline. In the unconstrained arm, all methods are near the task ceiling
(0.089--0.108 RMSE) and depth has no meaningful effect.

\subsection{Task-Loss Repair Fails Its Gate}

Figure~\ref{fig:ranking-fidelity} shows the canonical Path B Gate 0 result.
At depth 1, the task-loss scorer reaches $\rho=0.231$ against realized
posterior-risk reduction, with a 95\% bootstrap interval $[0.098,0.360]$.
This is positive but below the $\approx0.3$ proceed threshold. Correlation with
realized point-RMSE reduction is 0.249 $[0.130,0.371]$. The rankability ceiling
between realized posterior-risk and point-RMSE reductions is only 0.165
$[0.020,0.301]$.

The failure is not explained by one unlucky integration budget. With
independently resampled supports, depth-1 task-loss correlations are 0.278 at
256 samples and 0.262 at 1024 samples. The mean score-SE to between-candidate-SD
ratio falls from 0.316 to 0.162, but fidelity does not improve. A common prior
pool and deterministic systematic resampling yield the canonical 0.231 result.
Longer horizons also fail: depth 3 reaches 0.145 in the 256-sample diagnostic.

The round split identifies the break. Task-vs-smooth correlation is 0.055 at
the prior state, 0.305 after three observations, and 0.335 after six. Once data
concentrate the posterior, posterior-predictive utility becomes modestly useful.
Before data, however, the scorer averages over possible truths while each
realized ranking is conditional on one unknown draw. More rollouts estimate the
Bayesian average more precisely; they do not make it predict which prior draw
occurred. Selecting only the later rounds would pass, but that stratum was not
pre-registered and would omit the first decision faced by the deployed policy.

\subsection{Why The Stop Matters}

The proposed downstream method would have included the plain raw-history naive
query, greedy-EIG query, and LLM strategic roots, then defaulted to naive unless
another candidate exceeded it by one rollout standard error. That conservative
rule can control estimator noise, but it cannot repair a scorer whose rankings
are weakly related to realized gains. Gate 0 therefore prevents a costly and
statistically ambiguous policy sweep. No result in this paper claims that
task-loss arbitration beats naive or greedy EIG.

\section{Discussion}

The experiments expose two distinct failures. Entropy StrategyEIG has good
scorer-to-objective fidelity but poor objective-to-RMSE alignment. Expected
posterior RMSE fixes the semantic objective, yet its posterior-predictive values
still weakly rank truth-conditional outcomes, especially before observations.
The second failure is subtler: the utility can be Bayes-rational in expectation
without being a reliable per-instance arbiter among candidate LLM plans.

This distinction changes how LLM-BED systems should be evaluated. Reporting
only selected EIG or entropy drop can certify the simulator rather than the
task. Reporting only final RMSE can obscure whether failure comes from proposal
quality, score noise, or an unrankable endpoint. A useful diagnostic chain is:
estimated score to realized smooth objective, smooth objective to point task
loss, and finally policy improvement under paired deployment. Each link needs
its own uncertainty and stop rule.

The negative result does not show that task-aligned utilities are useless.
Later-round states cross the nominal correlation threshold, suggesting that
arbitration may become viable after posterior concentration. A future method
could explicitly abstain at early rounds and activate only after an online
fidelity or effective-sample-size criterion. That is a new, post-hoc hypothesis,
not a claim tested here.

\section{Limitations}

This is a workshop-scale diagnostic based on one model family and 60 probe
states from a limited number of paired trials. The standard-task Gate 0 uses an
exact analytic likelihood, so it isolates utility fidelity under a particle
posterior; open-ended LLM-BED settings must also contend with LLM-estimated
posterior error. The legacy records omit rollout posterior snapshots, requiring
a local fallback. Depth 1 avoids future-policy mismatch, but the common
100,000-particle pool, 256-particle support, and MCMC rejuvenation remain a
numerical approximation. Low late-round importance effective sample size is
reported rather than treated as exact posterior coverage.

Path A uses one deliberately constructed constrained environment family,
method-blind oracle selection, a robustness heatmap, and a limited number of
paired trials. Its 26B A4B runs use millions of tokens and include
forced-thinking exits. The minimum package lacks an MPC-style direct
action-sequence ablation; that remains future work. Most importantly, Gate 1 is
not missing positive evidence: it was deliberately not run after the
pre-registered Gate 0 failure. Therefore we make no performance claim for the
unimplemented arbitration policy and no claim that task-aligned utilities
transfer for free to LLM-estimated posteriors.

\section{Conclusion}

An LLM experimental-design scaffold can rank entropy reduction while failing
to rank localization accuracy. Replacing entropy with expected posterior RMSE
is principled but does not automatically repair the per-instance selection
link: our standard-task scorer remains below its pre-registered fidelity gate
after rollout and support diagnostics. The scientifically useful result is the
stop itself. Before asking whether deeper LLM plans beat naive or greedy design,
we should establish that their scores rank the realized task-facing quantity;
in this study, neither entropy nor its task-loss replacement clears that bar
robustly.
