\title{Can Language Models Evaluate Their Own Experimental Plans?}
\author{Hanshal Goyal}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Large language models can propose rich experimental strategies, but
non-myopic Bayesian experimental design also requires ranking those plans by
downstream information gain. In a locality-constrained location-finding task,
we test whether rollout-scored natural-language strategies predict realized
posterior information. A 26B A4B model passes this first-link diagnostic:
estimated scores correlate with realized entropy drop and truth-anchored
posterior quality, though point RMSE remains noisy. In a paired depth sweep,
depth-5 StrategyEIG improves over depth-1 and matched-compute myopic
StrategyEIG under movement constraints, but does not beat greedy EIG or naive
execution; an unconstrained contrast shows no depth effect.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Sequential experimental design often needs non-myopic decisions: a low-value
measurement now can move the experimenter into a region where future
measurements become valuable. Classical planners can exploit this structure
when the model, action space, and constraints are explicit. The harder question
for LLM-driven BED is whether a general-purpose language model can formulate
and evaluate reusable natural-language strategies without training a
task-specific planner.

This paper asks a deliberately narrow question: can LLM-generated strategies be
ranked by realized downstream information gain? Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A ranking-fidelity diagnostic for StrategyEIG, measuring whether
  rollout-estimated EIG ranks natural-language strategies by realized entropy
  reduction and truth-anchored posterior quality.
  \item A locality-constrained location-finding benchmark with a banked
  non-LLM oracle gap between greedy EIG and a depth-2 planner.
  \item A paired depth sweep with matched-compute myopic controls, separating
  non-myopic objective gains from extra rollout compute.
  \item A cost-vs-depth accounting that contrasts natural-language strategy
  rollout with brute-force LLM n-step expansion.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Bayesian experimental design.}
Deep adaptive design learns amortized policies for sequential BED
\citep{foster2021deep}, while classical online planners optimize explicit
transition-constrained design problems. We keep the probabilistic model
explicit but ask an LLM to generate and score strategy-level policies.

\paragraph{Constrained BED and informative path planning.}
Constrained location finding is close to informative path planning. COPEx is
especially adjacent \citep{guo2026constrained}: it studies
transition-constrained BED with scenario-tree lookahead. Our differentiation is
the natural-language strategy scaffold and ranking-fidelity diagnostic, not
novelty of the constrained-planning task.

\paragraph{LLM-driven experimental design.}
BED-LLM-style greedy and brute-force n-step question expansion provide a
natural baseline \citep{choudhury2025bedllm}. Brute-force n-step expansion can
be non-myopic, but its tree cost grows quickly with depth. StrategyEIG aims to
keep evaluation roughly linear in the number of candidate strategies and
rollout depth.

\section{Method}

\subsection{StrategyEIG}

At each round, the model proposes candidate natural-language strategies, each
paired with an executable root query. StrategyEIG evaluates these
strategy-level policies rather than expanding a full LLM action tree. The root
query is fixed for the first rollout step; later rollout queries condition on
the strategy text, simulated history, and analytical belief state.

The scorer uses the explicit location likelihood, shares rollout controls
across candidates, scores entropy reduction on a fixed common support, and
disables final LLM belief refresh during scoring. The selected strategy
executes only its root query; the posterior is updated from the realized
observation before the next BED round.

\subsection{Ranking-Fidelity Diagnostic}

For a belief state, we generate fixed candidate strategies and score them at
depths 2, 3, and 5. We then deploy each candidate under paired simulation seeds
and compare estimated score against realized entropy drop, truth log posterior
probability, and RMSE. Spearman correlation measures ranking fidelity, top-1
regret measures argmax loss, and trajectory distances check whether different
strategy texts actually induce different query paths.

\subsection{Paired Depth Sweep}

The final sweep shares hidden states, initial beliefs, and observation noise
across greedy EIG, naive baselines, StrategyEIG depths, and matched-compute
myopic controls. The constrained arm is the headline environment; the
unconstrained arm is the contrast case.

The primary endpoint is paired final truth log posterior probability.
Secondary endpoints are point RMSE, expected posterior RMSE, entropy, win
rates, and paired differences. Matched-compute myopic controls use the same
rollout budget but score first-step entropy reduction.

\section{Environment}

The headline task is a one-source branch-decoy location-finding environment
that can be read as informative path planning. A mobile sensor starts at the
origin, takes six measurements, and in the constrained arm obeys a maximum
movement radius. A finite-range local-bump signal keeps distant branches
indistinguishable until the sensor commits to moving toward one of them. With a
branch-decoy prior, locally attractive measurements can be less useful than
movement that positions the sensor for later discrimination.

The environment was selected method-blind with respect to StrategyEIG, using
only non-LLM oracle policies: grid greedy EIG, a depth-2 planner, non-adaptive
lawnmower coverage, and random walk. StrategyEIG was not in the tuning loop.
The constructed instance locates a planning-sensitive regime, not a universal
claim about source localization.

The banked non-LLM oracle check uses 120 paired trials. Greedy EIG reaches final
RMSE 0.5606, while a depth-2 planner reaches 0.1529. The planner win rate is
0.525, suggesting heavy-tailed improvements and motivating mean paired effects
with bootstrap intervals rather than only rank tests.

To scope the constructed-instance claim, we ran a 27-cell robustness heatmap
over signal lengthscale, movement radius, and noise, with 100 paired oracle
trials per cell. Figure~\ref{fig:env-robustness} shows planner-minus-greedy
RMSE; negative values favor lookahead. The Phase 4 operating point is among
the strongest cells, but nearby settings can be greedy-friendly.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Ranking Fidelity}

The Phase 1 26B A4B gate passes the information-ranking criterion, with
caveats. It used 20 trials, probe states after rounds 0, 3, and 6, eight
candidate strategies per probe, eight deployments per strategy/depth, and
rollout scoring depths 2, 3, and 5.

Figure~\ref{fig:ranking-fidelity} shows positive rank fidelity. Entropy-drop
Spearman correlations are $0.380\pm0.045$, $0.440\pm0.041$, and
$0.396\pm0.044$ at depths 2, 3, and 5. Truth-log-posterior correlations are
$0.371\pm0.040$, $0.373\pm0.045$, and $0.362\pm0.044$.

The caveat is that point-RMSE correlations are near zero
($-0.088\pm0.051$, $-0.080\pm0.045$, $-0.035\pm0.050$). We therefore treat
truth log posterior probability as the primary endpoint and RMSE as a noisier
localization readout. Top-1 entropy regret decreases from $0.326\pm0.048$ at
depth 2 to $0.211\pm0.044$ at depth 5.

\subsection{Depth Sweep}

The final package contains 30 paired constrained and 30 paired unconstrained
trials. In the constrained arm, StrategyEIG-d5 improves over d1 and
matched-compute myopic controls, but not over greedy EIG or naive. Final RMSE
means are 0.166 for naive, 0.412 for EIG, 0.542 for d5, 0.929 for d1, and
1.127 for d3. Truth-log-posterior means show the same partial-gain pattern:
naive $-1.484$, EIG $-1.672$, d5 $-1.982$, d1 $-2.532$, and d3 $-2.590$.

The unconstrained contrast is flat: final RMSE means are 0.089 for naive,
0.101 for EIG, 0.103 for d5, 0.103 for d1, and 0.108 for d3. This supports the
narrower outcome-playbook row: deeper non-myopic scoring improves StrategyEIG
under locality constraints, but the current strategy executor still trails
calibrated greedy or simple naive execution in this workshop-scale run.

\subsection{Cost Versus Depth}

StrategyEIG is meant to buy non-myopic evaluation without the exponential LLM
tree expansion of brute-force n-step EIG. The final package will report observed
token totals from the completed sweep logs using
\texttt{scripts/cost\_vs\_depth\_table.py}. For the support-grid MPP30 path,
the throughput-safe setting uses $B=6$ root candidates and $R=8$ paired
rollouts. A brute-force depth-$d$ LLM EIG proxy expands
$1+B+\cdots+B^{d-1}$ candidate sets per deployed decision and has $B^d$ leaf
action sequences. StrategyEIG uses one root strategy set per deployed decision
and $BRd$ simulated rollout steps. Thus increasing depth from 1 to 5 changes
StrategyEIG's rollout work linearly, from $48$ to $240$ simulated steps per
decision, while the brute-force candidate-set proxy grows from $1$ to $1{,}555$
root-equivalent LLM expansions per decision. The package also emits the
corresponding Markdown cost table for exact counts; Figure~\ref{fig:cost-depth}
shows the scaling visually.

\subsection{Qualitative Strategies}

Qualitative examples in the package show that natural-language strategies often
encode sensible local commitments, such as probing the most likely branch and
then refining around the observed high-signal area. The failure mode is also
visible: a strategy can describe the right branch-level intent while the
executed coordinates remain too coarse or arrive too late, leaving analytic EIG
or naive execution competitive on point RMSE.

\section{Limitations}

This is a workshop-scale study. The final sweep uses one constrained
environment family, one headline model family, and a limited number of paired
trials, so any positive result should be read as evidence that the StrategyEIG
scaffold can exploit a mapped planning-sensitive regime, not as a universal
claim about source localization. The environment is deliberately constructed:
finite-range sensing, movement constraints, and branch-decoy priors create a
setting where a non-LLM oracle planner beats greedy EIG. We reduce selection
bias by choosing the geometry using only method-blind non-LLM oracle policies
and by reporting the robustness heatmap in Figure~\ref{fig:env-robustness},
which shows that many nearby geometries remain greedy-friendly.

The language-model runs also carry systems-level caveats. Thinking-enabled
26B A4B inference has nontrivial forced-thinking-exit rates; final claims must
therefore report token usage and forced-exit counts alongside accuracy metrics.
Because the ranking-fidelity diagnostic finds truth-anchored information
metrics easier to rank than point RMSE, the paper treats RMSE as a secondary,
noisier localization readout rather than the sole arbiter of planning quality.
Finally, the minimum package does not include an MPC-style direct
action-sequence ablation. That ablation remains important future work for
separating the value of natural-language strategy abstraction from direct
LLM-generated action planning at matched compute.

\section{Conclusion}

The result is mixed but informative. Ranking fidelity is good enough to pass
the first-link gate, and depth-5 non-myopic StrategyEIG improves over
short-horizon and matched-compute myopic StrategyEIG in the constrained
branch-decoy environment. The stronger claim, however, is not supported:
StrategyEIG does not beat greedy EIG or naive execution, and the unconstrained
contrast shows no depth effect. We therefore view this as evidence for the
promise and current bottleneck of LLM-mediated BED: language models can rank
and express useful experimental plans, but calibrated execution remains the
limiting link.
