\begin{abstract}
Large language models can propose rich natural-language experimental
strategies, but non-myopic Bayesian experimental design requires more than
plausible plans: the model must rank those plans by their expected downstream
information gain. We study this question in a locality-constrained
location-finding environment designed to expose a greedy-planning gap through
movement cost, finite-range sensing, and branch-decoy source structure. Our
diagnostic first measures whether rollout-scored natural-language strategies
predict realized information gain. The current 26B A4B gate shows positive
rank fidelity for entropy and truth-anchored posterior quality, while point
RMSE remains noisy. We then pre-register a paired depth-sweep analysis comparing
greedy EIG, StrategyEIG depths 1/3/5, and matched-compute myopic controls.

\section{Introduction}

Sequential experimental design often needs non-myopic decisions: a low-value
measurement now can move the experimenter into a region where future
measurements become valuable. Classical planners can exploit this structure
when the model, action space, and constraints are explicit. The harder question
for LLM-driven BED is whether a general-purpose language model can formulate
and evaluate reusable natural-language strategies without training a
task-specific planner.

This paper asks a deliberately narrow question: can LLM-generated strategies be
ranked by their realized downstream information gain? We focus on a constrained
location-finding task where a non-LLM oracle shows that lookahead should matter.
The contribution is not a new robotics planner, but a diagnostic and
experimental scaffold for natural-language strategy planning.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A ranking-fidelity diagnostic for StrategyEIG, measuring whether
  rollout-estimated EIG ranks natural-language strategies by realized entropy
  reduction and truth-anchored posterior quality.
  \item A locality-constrained location-finding benchmark with a banked
  non-LLM oracle gap between greedy EIG and a depth-2 planner.
  \item A pre-registered paired depth sweep with matched-compute myopic
  controls, designed to separate non-myopic objective gains from extra rollout
  compute.
  \item A cost-vs-depth accounting that contrasts natural-language strategy
  rollout with brute-force LLM n-step expansion.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Bayesian experimental design.}
Deep adaptive design learns amortized policies for sequential BED in fixed
parametric spaces \citep{foster2021deep}, while classical online planners
optimize explicit transition-constrained design problems. Our setting keeps the
probabilistic model explicit but asks an LLM to generate and score
strategy-level policies.

\paragraph{Constrained BED and informative path planning.}
Constrained location finding is structurally close to informative path
planning. Guo et al.'s COPEx work on constrained BED via online planning is
especially adjacent \citep{guo2026constrained}: it studies
transition-constrained location finding and uses learned posterior/predictive
surrogates with scenario-tree lookahead. Our differentiation is the portable
natural-language strategy scaffold and its ranking-fidelity diagnostic, not
novelty of the constrained-planning task.

\paragraph{LLM-driven experimental design.}
BED-LLM-style greedy and brute-force n-step question expansion provide a
natural baseline \citep{choudhury2025bedllm}. Brute-force n-step expansion can
be non-myopic, but its tree cost grows quickly with depth. StrategyEIG aims to
keep evaluation roughly linear in the number of candidate strategies and
rollout depth.

\section{Method}

\subsection{StrategyEIG}

At each round, the model proposes a small set of candidate natural-language
strategies, each paired with an executable root query. A strategy is text such
as a conditional search plan: where to probe first, what observation regimes
would indicate, and where future probes should move under those regimes.
StrategyEIG evaluates these strategy-level policies rather than expanding a
full LLM question/action tree. For each candidate, the root query is fixed for
the first rollout step; later rollout queries are generated by conditioning the
LLM on the strategy text, the simulated history, and the current analytical
belief state.

The rollout scorer uses the explicit location likelihood, not an LLM posterior,
to update beliefs during evaluation. The configured scorer used for the gate
and final sweeps shares rollout random controls across candidate strategies,
scores entropy reduction on a fixed common support, and disables the final
LLM-based belief refresh during scoring. These choices reduce ranking noise and
make strategy comparisons paired within a belief state. The selected strategy
executes only its root query in the real environment; the posterior is then
updated from the realized observation before the next BED round.

\subsection{Ranking-Fidelity Diagnostic}

For a belief state, we generate a fixed set of candidate strategies and score
each candidate at depths 2, 3, and 5. We then deploy each candidate under paired
simulation seeds and compare estimated rollout score against realized entropy
drop, realized truth log posterior probability, and realized RMSE. Spearman
rank correlation measures whether rollout scores rank strategies correctly, and
top-1 regret measures the deployment-relevant error: how much realized
information is lost by choosing the estimator's argmax instead of the best
candidate in hindsight. We also measure strategy-execution fidelity by
comparing within-strategy and between-strategy query-trajectory distances; if
different strategy texts induce indistinguishable query paths, ranking fidelity
would not be actionable.

\subsection{Paired Depth Sweep}

The final sweep uses shared hidden states, initial beliefs, and observation
noise across greedy EIG, naive baselines, StrategyEIG depths, and
matched-compute myopic controls. This fixed-root pairing makes each trial a
within-instance comparison rather than an aggregate comparison over different
source draws. The constrained arm is the headline environment; the
unconstrained arm is a contrast case where the locality-induced planning gap
should be weaker.

The pre-registered primary endpoint is paired final truth log posterior
probability, comparing StrategyEIG depths against greedy EIG and against depth
1 when testing a depth effect. We report mean paired differences with bootstrap
confidence intervals over trials. Secondary endpoints are final point RMSE,
expected posterior RMSE, posterior entropy, per-trial win rates, and paired
difference distributions. Matched-compute myopic controls use the same rollout
budget but score strategies by first-step entropy reduction, isolating the
non-myopic objective from extra LLM compute.

\section{Environment}

The headline task is a one-source branch-decoy location-finding environment
that can be read as a small informative-path-planning problem. A mobile sensor
starts at the origin and may take six measurements. Consecutive measurements
are constrained by a maximum movement radius, representing travel cost or
limited actuation. Observations follow a finite-range local-bump signal rather
than the original long-range inverse-square signal, so distant branches remain
nearly indistinguishable until the sensor physically commits to moving toward
one of them. The source prior places probability mass on branch-like
alternatives, including decoys. Together these ingredients create the intended
non-myopic structure: a locally attractive measurement can be less useful than
movement that positions the sensor for later branch discrimination.

The environment was selected method-blind with respect to StrategyEIG. We tuned
geometry and signal parameters using only non-LLM oracle policies: grid greedy
EIG, a depth-2 receding-horizon planner, a non-adaptive lawnmower coverage
policy, and a random walk. StrategyEIG was not in the tuning loop. This matters
because many simple location geometries are greedy-friendly; the constructed
instance is meant to locate a planning-sensitive regime, not to claim that
lookahead is universally useful for source localization.

The banked non-LLM oracle check uses 120 paired trials. Greedy EIG reaches final
RMSE 0.5606, while a depth-2 planner reaches 0.1529. The planner win rate is
0.525, suggesting heavy-tailed improvements and motivating mean paired effects
with bootstrap intervals rather than only rank tests.

To scope the constructed-instance claim, we additionally ran a 27-cell
robustness sweep over signal lengthscale, movement radius, and observation
noise, with 100 paired oracle trials per cell. Figure~\ref{fig:env-robustness}
shows planner-minus-greedy final RMSE; negative values indicate that lookahead
helps. The Phase 4 operating point, lengthscale 0.5, radius 0.5, noise 0.15,
is among the strongest cells (planner--greedy final RMSE $=-0.175$). Nearby
settings often have weak or near-zero gaps, especially when the local-bump
signal is too short-ranged or too smooth. We therefore use the heatmap as scope
evidence: non-myopic traps exist in this environment family, but they occupy a
specific regime rather than the whole parameter space.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Ranking Fidelity}

The Phase 1 26B A4B gate passes the information-ranking criterion, with
caveats. The diagnostic used 20 trials, probe states after rounds 0, 3, and 6
(60 records total), eight candidate strategies per probe, eight deployments per
strategy/depth, and rollout scoring depths 2, 3, and 5. The configured scorer
uses common rollout controls, fixed-common analytical support, and no final LLM
belief refresh during scoring.

Figure~\ref{fig:ranking-fidelity} shows that rollout-estimated EIG ranks
strategies above chance by realized entropy drop: Spearman correlations are
$0.380\pm0.045$ at depth 2, $0.440\pm0.041$ at depth 3, and
$0.396\pm0.044$ at depth 5. Truth-anchored posterior quality is also
positively rank-aligned, with truth-log-posterior Spearman correlations
$0.371\pm0.040$, $0.373\pm0.045$, and $0.362\pm0.044$ at depths 2, 3, and 5.
This satisfies the first-link gate: the LLM rollout scorer can rank its own
natural-language strategies by downstream posterior information.

The caveat is that point-RMSE is not rankable in the same diagnostic. RMSE
Spearman correlations are near zero ($-0.088\pm0.051$, $-0.080\pm0.045$,
and $-0.035\pm0.050$). A realized-realized repair analysis also finds only weak
alignment between realized posterior-information gains and realized point-RMSE
gains. We therefore treat truth log posterior probability as the primary
deployment endpoint for the final sweep and report RMSE as an important but
noisier localization readout. Top-1 entropy regret decreases from
$0.326\pm0.048$ at depth 2 to $0.211\pm0.044$ at depth 5, suggesting that
deeper scoring is useful for choosing the deployed argmax strategy even though
this diagnostic alone does not prove a monotonic deployment depth effect.

\subsection{Cost Versus Depth}

StrategyEIG is meant to buy non-myopic evaluation without the exponential LLM
tree expansion of brute-force n-step EIG. The final package will report observed
token totals from the completed sweep logs using
\texttt{scripts/cost\_vs\_depth\_table.py}. Before those runs land, the
algorithmic comparison is already fixed by the pre-registered Path A settings:
the LLM generates $B=10$ candidate root strategies per decision and evaluates
them with $R=16$ paired rollouts. A brute-force depth-$d$ LLM EIG proxy expands
$1+B+\cdots+B^{d-1}$ candidate sets per deployed decision and has $B^d$ leaf
action sequences. StrategyEIG uses one root strategy set per deployed decision
and $BRd$ simulated rollout steps. Thus increasing depth from 1 to 5 changes
StrategyEIG's rollout work linearly, from $160$ to $800$ simulated steps per
decision, while the brute-force candidate-set proxy grows from $1$ to $11{,}111$
root-equivalent LLM expansions per decision.

\section{Limitations}

This is a workshop-scale study. The final sweep uses one constrained
environment family, one headline model family, and a limited number of paired
trials, so any positive result should be read as evidence that the StrategyEIG
scaffold can exploit a mapped planning-sensitive regime, not as a universal
claim about source localization. The environment is deliberately constructed:
finite-range sensing, movement constraints, and branch-decoy priors create a
setting where a non-LLM oracle planner beats greedy EIG. We reduce selection
bias by choosing the geometry using only method-blind non-LLM oracle policies
and by reporting the robustness heatmap in Figure~\ref{fig:env-robustness},
which shows that many nearby geometries remain greedy-friendly.

The language-model runs also carry systems-level caveats. Thinking-enabled
26B A4B inference has nontrivial forced-thinking-exit rates; final claims must
therefore report token usage and forced-exit counts alongside accuracy metrics.
Because the ranking-fidelity diagnostic finds truth-anchored information
metrics easier to rank than point RMSE, the paper treats RMSE as a secondary,
noisier localization readout rather than the sole arbiter of planning quality.
Finally, the minimum package does not include an MPC-style direct
action-sequence ablation. That ablation remains important future work for
separating the value of natural-language strategy abstraction from direct
LLM-generated action planning at matched compute.
