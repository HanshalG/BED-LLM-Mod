# DiscoveryWorld Native Opportunity Audit Result

## Decision

Do not spend OpenRouter credit on a DiscoveryWorld policy run yet.

The deterministic wrapper makes paired replay viable, and the official
scorecards provide unusually good external endpoints. However, none of the
eight native Challenge tasks supplies both:

1. a defensible latent-law prior and strict myopic-versus-lookahead opportunity;
   and
2. an open hypothesis space whose generation or likelihood genuinely requires
   an LLM.

The tasks contain real scientific prerequisites, but their hidden mechanisms
are compact source-defined families. Building an exact BED model from those
families would make the LLM an action-language convenience rather than the
belief machinery. Hiding the families from a classical baseline while exposing
them implicitly to an LLM would not be a fair irreducibility claim.

## Frozen Requirements

This audit applies the successor requirements recorded before deterministic
replay:

- use a scientific-law prior rather than scenario-seed identity;
- preserve open semantic hypothesis and experiment proposal;
- demonstrate a strict oracle lower-immediate/higher-terminal root difference
  before any model call;
- evaluate with exact simulator observations and task endpoints; and
- avoid replacing the native action space with a small hand-written macro menu.

The replay pass satisfied the simulator requirement only.

## Theme Audit

| Challenge theme | Native hidden mechanism | Why it does not clear the headline gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Combinatorial Chemistry | A mixture over four named substances with two or more components and small integer proportions | Exact compositions and dispenser effects form a compact combinatorial search; a classical active learner can enumerate them. |
| Archaeology Dating | One of four radioisotope channels tracks age; three known artifacts calibrate the channel before three unknown artifacts are ranked | The prerequisite is real, but the core latent variable is a four-way channel choice with a fixed monotone rule. |
| Plant Nutrients | One of four Boolean rule templates over five nutrients at three discrete levels | The complete rule grammar is explicitly finite and enumerable from source. |
| Reactor Lab | One of five instruments is the key dimension; resonance follows a quadratic with bounded integer coefficients | Measurements and delayed fitting are substantive, but the law family and sensor set are explicit enough for exact symbolic enumeration/regression. |
| Lost in Translation | Fixed alien dictionary; the requested target is one of 3 items, 7 colors, and 5 counts | The native target space has only 105 combinations. Tool-use prerequisites do not make the belief support open. |
| Space Sick | One of five instruments detects one contaminated item among three foods | The latent support is at most 15 instrument-item pairs. |
| Proteomics | One outlier species among five, identified from two- or three-dimensional protein measurements | The scientific endpoint is exact, but the hypothesis is a five-way outlier label. |
| Rocket Science | Continuous planet parameters inferred through fixed textbook equations, followed by selection among three generated fuels | The action chain is long and consequential, but the released equations and three-way fuel decision support a direct classical system-identification planner. |

## What Remains Valuable

DiscoveryWorld remains a good future **execution** benchmark once an
LLM-native BED method has already been validated elsewhere. A defensible use
would let the LLM propose executable scientific hypotheses and low-level
experiments while a simulator-grounded evaluator checks outcomes. It should not
be the next place where the project tries to prove that the LLM is
irreducible.

A scientifically distinct successor would need to add a prospectively defined
open law generator or natural-language latent-world distribution whose support
is not available to an enumerative baseline. That would be a new benchmark
construction, not a result on unmodified native DiscoveryWorld.
