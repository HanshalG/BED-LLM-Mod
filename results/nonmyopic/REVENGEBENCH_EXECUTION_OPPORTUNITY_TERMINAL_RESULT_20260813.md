## Frozen decision

The prospectively frozen stage required at least two qualifying arenas. A
qualifying arena had to change the exact depth-two first probe from
compute-matched receding myopic at every beta, gain at least `0.01` nats in every
changed case, and pass held-out endpoint separation.

The stage cannot pass:

| Arena | Status | Consequence |

| BattleSnake | Complete, exact, non-qualifying | Changed order at beta 0.5/1.0 has exactly zero utility gain; beta 2.0 agrees |

Once BattleSnake was non-qualifying and Halite failed, no HuskyBench outcome

## Interpretation

RevengeBench is a useful negative source-admission lesson, not evidence that
non-myopic LLM BED fails in general:

- deterministic executable-policy mechanics can be recovered, but the released
  population contains heterogeneous or invalid game interfaces that entrypoint
  existence and compilation do not detect;
- BattleSnake's selected probes provide nonzero information, yet adaptive depth
  two only reorders the same pair and gains no terminal information;
- no LLM semantic calibration or paid endpoint opened, so this experiment says
  nothing about an LLM planner's efficacy or belief quality.

The route is closed under its exact target/probe cohort, seeds, and gates. Do not
repair or replace the observed invalid probe, lower the two-arena threshold, or
reuse private trajectories for a successor. A future executable-policy benchmark
must source-audit the full native handshake with a minimal completed trajectory,
not just source shape and compilation, before freezing opportunity probes.

## Paper consequence

The paper headline remains unchanged. RevengeBench may be mentioned only as a
