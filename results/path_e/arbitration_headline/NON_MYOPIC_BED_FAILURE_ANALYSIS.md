## Executive Finding

This is not primarily a rollout-count, planning-depth, thinking-budget, or model-size
problem. The current stack violates the conditions needed for non-myopic Bayesian
experimental design at several levels:

1. The evaluated transition and terminal model is not faithful enough to identify the
   better policy.
2. The acquisition function optimizes information about free-form cause-and-remedy
   strings, while the task rewards resolving the issue quickly. Information and task
   value are not the same objective here.
3. The simulated two-step process does not match deployed execution: it uses fixed
   support, synthetic answer text, and nonterminal branches, while deployment refreshes
   and prunes support, sees free-text replies, and may terminate.
4. The belief state is an incoherent hybrid of history-conditioned proposal generation
   and full-history likelihood reweighting, over a semantically duplicated and
   unobserved support.
5. Likelihoods are single JSON probability judgements over jointly generated answer
   spaces, not calibrated response probabilities or token-logit estimates.
6. The released customer-service task does not enforce the prerequisite gates that were
   assumed to create a non-myopic advantage. A correct remedy can be proposed at turn
   one.

In one sentence: **the implementation is doing non-myopic information acquisition in an
unstable text model, not non-myopic task planning in the environment that produces the
reported reward.** More rollouts would estimate that wrong quantity more precisely.

### Arbitration did not identify useful overrides

- EIG overrode the native candidate on 113/222 turns (50.9%).
- The native candidate ranked first by the EIG model on only 61/222 turns; it ranked
  second 80 times and third 81 times. The model therefore considered the better
  candidate-0 policy inferior on 72.5% of turns.
- There were 47 task-level first divergences from candidate 0. On the divergent turn,
  the selected action alone resolved four tasks and candidate 0 alone resolved four;
  neither resolved 39. The immediate counterfactual is exactly balanced.
- Downstream from those first divergences, arbitration won 6 tasks, lost 12, and tied
  29 on censored turns. The score did not expose the delayed harm.
- Mean score gaps were almost indistinguishable across task outcomes: 0.0949 for wins,
  0.0866 for losses, and 0.0950 for ties. Mean excess above the alleged one-SE margin
  was 0.0652, 0.0458, and 0.0556, respectively. The threshold is not calibrated to
  override quality.

Several losses show the acquisition mismatch directly:

| Task and turn | Native candidate 0 | EIG override |
|---|---|---|
| Rice cooker, task 10 turn 3 | Clean the visibly dirty heating plate; resolved | Run a water-boiling test |
| TV audio, task 12 turn 3 | Select `TV Speakers`; resolved | Power-cycle the TV |
| Kitchen display, task 23 turn 4 | Reboot the device; resolved | Clear application cache |
| Laptop wireless, task 35 turn 1 | Check Airplane Mode; resolved | Inspect Device Manager |

These are not subtle failures of depth. The task-aligned native proposal already
contains the successful action, and pure EIG deliberately replaces it with an action
that promises more discrimination among hypotheses.

### The answer-space bottleneck causes stale beliefs at decisive moments

On task 10, the customer reported that the pot was flat but that burnt rice and crumbs
covered the heating plate. The generated outcomes were only "pot is seated flat and
clean", "pot is misaligned or obstructed", and uncertainty. The reply did not map, so
`update_belief_state` returned the old belief unchanged. On the next turn, EIG overrode
the exact cleaning remedy with the water test by a margin of 0.04397 against a threshold
of 0.04366.

This is the complete failure chain in one example:

1. The real reply contains the decisive clue.
2. The generated categorical answer space cannot represent the conjunction.
3. The mapper rejects it.
4. The belief update discards it for the current decision.
5. The stale EIG score selects a diagnostic over the exact remedy.

Eleven of the 113 overrides occurred immediately after an unclean mapping. Even clean
mapping can discard useful free-text detail because the update consumes the category,
not the full reply, at that step.

### Development performance did not replicate

The ten-task development arbitration result was 6 wins, 0 losses, and 4 ties against
thinking naive, with a mean delta of -1.2 censored turns. Held out, it became 6 wins,
16 losses, and 28 ties, with a mean delta of +0.44. Best-N moved from 4/1/5 and -0.9
turns on the same ten development tasks to 11/12/27 and +0.28 held out.

The development arbitration comparison also lacked the candidate-0 causal control and
mixed stochastic proposal quality with selection quality. The held-out control shows
that the selector itself did not add value. This is consistent with a small-development
set winner's curse and prompt/proposal sensitivity, not a robust BED mechanism.

### Full two-step search was both misdirected and prohibitively expensive

In the invalid ten-task Step-1 diagnostic, full two-step EIG resolved 2/10 versus 3/10
for one-step EIG, with 0 wins, 1 loss, and 9 ties. It made 70,004 requests versus 3,365
and cost $5.157 versus $0.253: approximately 20.8x the requests and 20.4x the cost.

The cost explosion follows directly from root candidates x root outcomes x branch
follow-up candidates x hypotheses. It did not buy task planning; it bought a larger
search over future information scores.

## Endpoint and Transition Validity Fail Before Method Evaluation

The known fatal contradiction is task 13: replacing/refilling the empty receipt-paper
roll is the exact private remedy, but the best-N simulator said the change did not help.
There are also clear action-credit failures in transcripts that were not the original
manual-audit stopping point:

- Task 10 arbitration deferred cleaning, asked about warranty, and the customer then
  spontaneously cleaned the plate and reported success. The run finally terminated on
  an offer to send a maintenance guide.
- Development task 5 asked the driver to test power with a multimeter. The simulated
  driver independently wiggled and secured the loose connector, fixing the lights, and
  the full-conversation judge credited the policy.

The released Paprika paper explicitly warns that open-ended LLM environments can be
hacked and reports residual errors even after a judge. It used temperature 0.0 for the
environment and judge to improve determinism. Every current Paprika config uses
`answer_temperature: 0.7`.

The adapter also combines terminal signals as
`customer_goal OR full_conversation_judge_goal`. This permits either fallible component
to terminate a run. More importantly, the full-conversation judge can credit a remedy
performed spontaneously by the customer instead of caused by the latest agent action.
The transition model therefore violates basic action attribution.

This invalidity does not explain away the selector's exact-remedy overrides, but it
means resolution counts cannot distinguish policies reliably. Endpoint repair is a
prerequisite, not a post-hoc robustness check.

## Code-Level Failure Analysis

### 1. The optimized objective is wrong for troubleshooting

`methods/categorical_eig.py:20-31` correctly computes

```text
I(H; Y | action, history).
```

`CategoricalEIG` selects its argmax. `FullTwoStepCategoricalEIG` adds immediate EIG to
the expected maximum second-step EIG. Neither method contains:

- probability of resolving the issue;
- reward for a correct remedy;
- a turn or action cost;
- a stopping decision;
- a penalty for an infeasible customer action.

Consider two actions under any nondegenerate belief. A remedy that resolves with
probability 0.9 under every hypothesis has EIG zero because its outcome does not
distinguish hypotheses. A diagnostic that reveals the hypothesis perfectly has high
EIG but cannot resolve the issue. The current method must choose the diagnostic even on
the last turn. That behavior is mathematically correct for its objective and wrong for
the benchmark.

This mismatch is weaker in 20 Questions because identifying the hidden hypothesis is
the terminal task. It is fundamental in Paprika because questions acquire information
while remedies intervene on and terminate the world.

### 2. The latent hypothesis is not a sufficient state

A Paprika hypothesis is a free-form sentence containing a cause and remedy. But remedy
actions change the physical state, and customer replies reveal whether an action was
performed, feasible, or successful. The correct model is closer to

```text
p(next_world_state, reply, terminal | world_state, cause, action).
```

The implementation only represents a distribution over cause/remedy text and leaves
the evolving world state implicit in the LLM prompt. As a result, it is not a Markov
belief state and cannot support a coherent Bellman recursion.

### 3. The belief update double-uses history for newly proposed hypotheses

At each clean observation, `env.py:501-555`:

1. generates new hypotheses conditioned on the complete observed history;
2. filters the merged support using that history;
3. resets every survivor to a uniform prior;
4. multiplies every survivor by LLM likelihoods for the complete history;
5. prunes to the top 24.

New hypotheses are therefore proposed because they fit the data and then scored again
on the same data, without proposal probabilities or importance correction. This is
neither a Bayesian update on fixed support nor BED-LLM's uniform sample-then-filter
approximation. Retained and newly generated hypotheses also have different proposal
histories but are treated as exchangeable prior samples.

Additional support problems:

- deduplication is exact case-insensitive string matching, not semantic clustering;
- paraphrases of one remedy can receive multiple support slots and entropy mass;
- there is no explicit `other` hypothesis;
- pruning can irreversibly remove the true remedy class;
- support, probabilities, filter decisions, and semantic truth coverage were not saved.

`true_solution_exact_mass` was zero on every logged final belief in both arbitration
and best-N. That metric only checks exact string equality, so it cannot tell whether the
truth was absent or represented by a paraphrase. It is therefore not a posterior-collapse
diagnostic.

### 4. The response likelihood is uncalibrated probabilistic introspection

For each hypothesis-action pair, the non-thinking evaluation model receives a prompt
asking it to emit a JSON probability dictionary over 3-5 natural-language outcomes.
There is one completion at temperature 0.0, no ensemble, no token-logit extraction,
and no calibration set.

The parser silently assigns zero probability to omitted outcome keys and, if the entire
row has zero mass, assigns probability one to the uncertainty outcome. Those repairs
keep jobs alive but alter the acquisition function in ways that are neither measured
nor represented in the arbitration threshold.

The action and its answer space are generated jointly. Candidate actions can therefore
change both the experiment and the granularity/cardinality of the variable on which
they are scored. Although mutual information is comparable across valid observation
models, an uncalibrated model can win by proposing crisp or finely partitioned outcome
labels that the real customer and mapper do not obey.

BED-LLM instead emphasizes restricted answer tokens and direct logits whenever
possible. Its estimator is only as meaningful as the induced joint distribution. Our
JSON self-reports are a materially noisier substitute.

### 5. The deployed update and simulated update are different algorithms

In full two-step scoring (`methods/categorical_eig.py:74-157`), a branch:

- retains the current fixed hypothesis support;
- applies one categorical Bayes reweighting;
- appends the generated outcome label as if it were the customer's reply;
- generates branch follow-ups from that synthetic history.

During real execution, a branch:

- receives free-form customer text;
- maps it through a fallible answer-space judge;
- may leave the belief unchanged on a mapping failure;
- generates, filters, reweights, and prunes hypotheses;
- may terminate.

BED-LLM explicitly identifies alignment between the EIG model and the actual belief
update as a central requirement. Here the planner evaluates one state transition and
deploys another.

### 6. Every simulated branch is forced to continue

`env.py:878-885` constructs every synthetic branch with `goal_reached=False`, including
an outcome such as "problem resolved after cleaning". Full two-step search therefore
assigns an additional future information reward after a terminal remedy and pays no
opportunity cost for asking another question. This alone prevents the score from being
a valid finite-horizon task value.

### 7. Branch proposal noise is maximized, not controlled

Each root-outcome branch samples its own follow-up candidates and takes the maximum
follow-up EIG. Root actions are consequently compared using different random candidate
sets. There are no common random numbers or repeated estimates of the root score. The
maximum of noisy branch candidates introduces optimizer's-curse bias, and depth adds
more opportunities for a lucky high score.

The branch text is also the terse synthetic outcome label, not a realistic reply. The
LLM follow-up policy is thus evaluated under a prompt distribution different from the
one it sees after deployment.

### 8. The arbitration "one-SE" rule has no sampling interpretation

`categorical_eig_standard_error` computes the weighted variance of per-hypothesis
information contributions and divides by an effective support size. But the hypotheses
are not independent draws from a calibrated posterior: they are correlated,
history-conditioned LLM generations that have been filtered, duplicated semantically,
reweighted, and pruned.

The threshold also treats candidate score errors as independent even though candidates
share the same support and model. It ignores likelihood-elicitation error, support
proposal error, answer-space error, and candidate-generation variability. Empirically,
its margin does not separate good from bad overrides. It should not be called a
standard error or used as a confidence rule.

### 9. The experiment assumed, rather than established, a planning gap

The released customer-service tasks contain a one-line symptom and one-line private
remedy. The free-text action space permits the correct remedy immediately, and the
environment does not encode unavailable actions, prerequisite locks, or resource
constraints. Any apparent prerequisite chain is a language-model convention, not a
benchmark transition rule.

For many information objectives with diminishing returns, greedy selection is already
near-optimal. Non-myopic gains require complementarity, gating, or a task utility whose
future value differs structurally from immediate EIG. We never measured an oracle
greedy-to-non-myopic gap on Paprika, so depth had no established gap to recover.

### 10. The saved artifacts cannot diagnose the claimed Bayesian mechanism

`save_artifacts` records chosen actions, replies, answer mappings, and selection extras,
but omits:

- generated and retained hypotheses per turn;
- semantic clusters and truth coverage;
- posterior probabilities, entropy, and effective support size;
- candidate likelihood matrices;
- filter decisions;
- simulated branch beliefs and terminal probabilities;
- predicted task values.

Consequently, posterior collapse, support loss, likelihood calibration, and branch-vs-
deployment divergence cannot be measured from completed runs. The main Bayesian state
of the Bayesian method was not logged.

## What the Literature Says Works

### BED-LLM

[BED-LLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21184) is strong evidence for greedy
LLM-assisted BED under the right formulation, not evidence that arbitrary EIG should
improve any interactive agent. Its important ingredients are:

- define the target quantity precisely;
- use a constrained answer space where uncertainty is meaningful;
- construct a coherent prior-likelihood joint;
- sample diverse hypotheses and reject those with low likelihood under observations;
- retain consistent hypotheses across turns;
- use the full conditional response entropy, preferably from token logits;
- align the acquisition model with the deployed update;
- avoid hypothesis-conditioned question generation when hypotheses are complex and
  overlapping because it can overfit the sampled support.

The paper also warns that entropy over raw representations can be meaningless when
many representations have the same task output. Cause-and-remedy prose has exactly
this problem: several strings can encode one intervention, while one string can bundle
multiple causal distinctions irrelevant to the remedy decision.

Our implementation shares the high-level generate-score-update loop but departs from
these load-bearing details. In particular, best-N generation conditions on overlapping
free-text hypotheses, the likelihood comes from JSON introspection rather than logits,
and the simulated and deployed updates differ.

### DAD and policy-based sequential BED

[Deep Adaptive Design](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/foster21a.html) trains a
history-to-design policy on complete simulated trajectories to maximize total-history
mutual information. It requires an evaluable likelihood and enough simulations to
learn the policy before deployment. DAD is not an online two-step expansion of greedy
EIG, and our `FullTwoStepCategoricalEIG` is not a DAD analogue.

DAD also does not solve the utility mismatch automatically. If its latent target were
our free-form cause/remedy string, it would efficiently optimize the same wrong target.
For troubleshooting, either the target must be changed to a decision-relevant quantity
or the policy must optimize terminal task return. The
[RL formulation of sequential BED](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/blau22a.html)
is the more natural template when designs are discrete, the simulator is a black box,
and rewards include task outcomes.

### Goal- and prediction-oriented design

[Prediction-Oriented Bayesian Active Learning](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/bickfordsmith23a.html)
shows why information about model parameters can be inferior to information about the
predictions that matter downstream.
[Goal-Oriented Sequential BED](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07359) extends the same
principle to non-myopic policies: target a user-specified quantity of interest, not the
entire latent model. This maps cleanly to troubleshooting. The relevant quantity is the
effectiveness of candidate remedies or the optimal terminal decision, not every textual
distinction among possible causes.

### Learned predictive simulators

[Adaptive Elicitation of Latent Information Using Natural Language](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04204)
meta-trains a predictive response model on historical trajectories before using it for
lookahead. Its most relevant ablation found that planning with the base LLM reduced
accuracy by almost 15% on hard questions, while planning helped after meta-training.
The paper's own bound stresses that simulator-planning gains transfer only when the
simulator is close to the real response distribution.

This is the closest literature analogue to our negative result. We used an off-the-shelf
LLM both to invent the latent support and to self-report branch probabilities, then
maximized through that model. Deeper search amplifies model bias unless the predictive
model is calibrated first.

### Paprika itself

[Paprika](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17543) does not train curiosity with an
information-gain objective. It samples many complete interaction trajectories and
prefers trajectories that succeed in fewer turns, then uses SFT and multi-turn DPO/RPO.
That is amortized task-return optimization. Its success is evidence for training on
task-level trajectories, not for pure EIG over root-cause strings.

Paprika also acknowledges residual LLM-environment hacking. That limitation becomes
more damaging for planning because the same transition errors are used both to select
actions and to evaluate them.

### Inference-time tree methods

[Uncertainty of Thoughts](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03271) and
[Feedback-Aware MCTS](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/bc634a1a1203d0b10d33ededea67e4d5-Abstract-Conference.html)
show that tree search can help LLM information seeking, but their favorable setup is
materially more constrained: a possibility set, mostly binary partitions, question-only
actions, a terminal target-identification phase, and in the latter case reuse of
successful historical paths. These ingredients reduce the world-model and credit-
assignment burden that breaks the current Paprika planner.

## The Correct First-Principles Formulation

Paprika troubleshooting should be modeled as a Bayes-adaptive control problem, not a
pure measurement problem.

Let `z` be the hidden cause/remedy class, `s_t` the mutable world and interaction state,
`b_t(z, s_t)` the current belief, `a_t` a diagnostic or remedy action, and `y_t` its
outcome. The transition model is

```text
p(y_t, s_(t+1), terminal | z, s_t, a_t).
```

For reward `r` that values resolution and charges turns/actions, the finite-horizon
recursion is

```text
Q_t(b, a) = E[r(b, a, y) + 1[not terminal] V_(t+1)(Update(b, a, y))]
V_t(b)    = max_a Q_t(b, a).
```

This single equation fixes four current errors: remedies receive direct value,
diagnostics receive only downstream value, terminal branches stop, and turns/actions
have a cost.

If a BED-specific information objective is desired, use a decision-relevant target such
as

```text
Z = identity or success vector of the optimal remedy,
```

not the full cause prose. A one-step diagnostic value can be expressed as expected
value of sample information:

```text
EVSI(q) = E_y[max_r E[U(r, z) | y, q]] - max_r E[U(r, z)] - cost(q).
```

Remedy actions are scored by immediate expected success plus continuation after a
failure. The non-myopic extension recursively plans later diagnostics and remedies.

## Recommended Replacement Architecture

### 1. Repair the environment before the method

- Set customer and judge temperature to 0.0 for scientific runs.
- Separate action transition from terminal scoring. A customer reply may describe an
  observation, but terminal reward must be attributed to the latest agent action.
- Score a latest remedy/diagnosis against the private solution with a strict semantic
  checker; require customer and terminal judge agreement rather than an OR.
- Reject replies in which the customer performs an unrequested remedy or reveals a new
  action outcome not caused by the agent.
- Add executable tests for every private remedy: exact remedy succeeds, unrelated
  remedies fail, diagnostic questions cannot terminate, and no customer self-repair is
  credited.

This is a new simulator/evaluation study and would require a fresh preregistration. It
cannot retroactively validate the current headline.

### 2. Use typed action semantics

Generate actions in two explicit classes:

- `diagnostic(question, answer_schema, feasibility)`;
- `intervention(remedy_class, expected_effect, feasibility)`.

Use fixed canonical response labels where possible:

- diagnostics: `A/B/C/D/unknown`, with natural-language descriptions attached;
- interventions: `resolved/unchanged/unable`.

The model should score label logits, not invent and then self-score arbitrary outcome
strings. Candidate generation may remain an LLM strength, but the probabilistic
interface must be controlled.

### 3. Make the belief representation decision-relevant and coherent

- Canonicalize generated causes into semantic remedy/effect classes.
- Cluster paraphrases before assigning mass.
- Include an explicit `other` class.
- Log semantic coverage of the private remedy during evaluation, without exposing it
  to the policy.
- Choose one update semantics and use it everywhere:
  - fixed-support Bayesian reweighting with an explicit likelihood; or
  - BED-LLM-style history-conditioned sampling, likelihood-threshold rejection, and a
    uniform distribution over retained samples.
- Do not propose a hypothesis from the history and then multiply it by the same history
  unless proposal probabilities are accounted for.
- Use the identical update in real and simulated branches.

### 4. Calibrate the response/world model before search

Use Paprika's training split to create held-out tuples of `(state, action, outcome,
terminal)` and measure:

- log loss and Brier score;
- calibration error for resolution probability;
- answer-schema coverage;
- semantic truth-support coverage;
- branch terminal precision and recall;
- consistency under paraphrased hypotheses/actions.

At minimum, use repeated model samples or a model ensemble to measure uncertainty. A
stronger path is to meta-train a lightweight response model or critic on train-split
trajectories while keeping the base LLM frozen. This follows the lesson from adaptive
elicitation and retains an LLM-based, amortized sequential-design system, though it is
no longer strictly training-free.

### 5. Establish a real non-myopic gap before LLM search

Construct a small typed abstraction from training tasks and solve it exactly by dynamic
programming. Compare:

- greedy one-step task value;
- optimal depth-two task value;
- native policy.

Proceed only if early actions genuinely change the value or availability of later
actions. If the benchmark allows the remedy immediately and shows no oracle gap, it is
not a valid environment for a non-myopic headline, regardless of LLM quality.

### 6. Use task-value MCTS or an amortized policy, not exhaustive EIG trees

For a training-free first implementation:

- generate one shared root candidate set;
- use the native thinking policy as the rollout policy;
- simulate typed outcomes with the calibrated world model;
- stop terminal branches;
- score cumulative task return, not cumulative entropy;
- use common random numbers across root candidates;
- estimate score uncertainty from independent rollout/model replicates;
- cap at depth two until ranking fidelity passes;
- cache shared states and use progressive widening rather than full expansion.

For the stronger DAD-like implementation:

- train a history-to-action policy or value critic over Paprika train tasks;
- optimize expected terminal resolution minus turn/action costs, or information about
  the optimal remedy if actions are restricted to pure questions;
- evaluate the frozen policy on unseen tasks;
- use the LLM for semantic state/action encoding and proposal generation, with the
  learned policy/critic providing non-myopic value.

This preserves the research goal: non-myopic sequential BED with LLMs. It stops asking
an off-the-shelf LLM to be an uncalibrated prior, likelihood, simulator, mapper, judge,
and planner simultaneously.

## Falsifiable Validation Gates

No large LLM sweep should precede these gates.

### Gate 0: endpoint and action credit

- 100% of exact private remedies terminate in a deterministic dev audit.
- 0% of clearly unrelated remedies terminate.
- 0 diagnostic-only actions or customer-initiated remedies receive terminal credit.
- Repeated temperature-0 transitions are identical or explicitly modeled as stochastic.

### Gate 1: support and update

- Semantic private-remedy coverage at least 95% before pruning on dev tasks.
- Semantic duplicate rate below 5%, with an explicit `other` class retained.
- Every deployed update is reproducible from logged support, likelihoods, and history.
- Simulated branch updates and deployed updates are identical for the same typed outcome.

### Gate 2: likelihood and world-model calibration

- Answer-schema coverage at least 98% on held-out dev transitions.
- Log loss and Brier score beat uniform and prompt-only baselines.
- Resolution probabilities are calibrated by action type and task family.
- No silent zero-key or all-zero repair is included without a logged rate and ablation.

### Gate 3: one-step task-value ranking

For shared candidate sets, counterfactually execute or deterministically judge every
root candidate on dev states. Require:

- positive rank correlation between predicted `Q_1` and realized task return;
- lower top-1 regret than native candidate 0;
- exact-remedy candidates are not overridden by diagnostics on the final turn;
- gains hold with the same candidate set, simulator seed, and answer schema.

This is the task-return analogue of the location ranking-fidelity gate. Entropy
correlation alone is insufficient.

### Gate 4: oracle non-myopic gap

On the exact typed abstraction, depth-two optimal value must exceed greedy value by a
predeclared practically meaningful amount. The gap must arise from released task
structure, not an engineered restriction added after seeing results.

### Gate 5: depth-two ranking fidelity

- Predicted depth-two root values correlate with counterfactual depth-two returns.
- Depth two beats depth one on paired dev states.
- A matched-compute myopic control does not explain the gain.
- Score uncertainty comes from independent rollouts/model draws, and root candidates
  use common random numbers.

### Gate 6: frozen held-out evaluation

Only after Gates 0-5 pass: preregister task IDs, endpoint, simulator, utility, candidate
sharing, retry rules, and analysis; then run paired held-out arms once.

## What Not to Try Next

- Do not increase depth or rollout count in the current implementation. It reduces
  Monte Carlo noise around a biased model and objective.
- Do not treat a larger thinking budget as a probability-calibration fix. The likelihood
  path is non-thinking JSON introspection, and the main errors are structural.
- Do not tune the one-SE margin. It is not a valid standard error, and held-out margins
  did not separate wins from losses.
- Do not refresh more free-form hypotheses without semantic canonicalization and a
  coherent proposal/update rule.
- Do not claim a non-myopic benefit until an oracle gap and depth-two task-value ranking
  both pass.

## Bottom Line

The current negative result is informative. It says that putting a deeper tree around
BED-LLM-shaped components is not enough. Non-myopic LLM-BED needs three things that the
